# Furniture construction and scale

Review every candidate beside the measured 5.74-stud avatar in Roblox, from the normal play camera. Blender units represent studs; the authoring scene uses 0.28 meters per unit. The avatar comparison is the final size check.

| Feature | Initial target in studs | Check |
|---|---:|---|
| Chair or sofa seat top | 1.8–2.3 | Feet reach the floor; seat fits the avatar's hips |
| Dining/work surface | 2.7–3.2 | Knee space and chair clearance remain visible |
| Door opening | 4.8 wide × 8 high | Normal avatar walks through without jumping |
| Cottage interior | 36 wide × 32 deep × 10.8 high | Leave the entry and inward door swing free |

These are starting ranges, not a reason to flatten every silhouette into the same proportions. Loungers, stools and sculptural pieces can differ when their intended use explains the difference.

Every foot must touch the floor and intersect its load-bearing frame. Chair legs attach to the seat or rail; lamp stems attach to a base and shade; shelves have brackets, legs or a credible wall mount. Do not use isolated balls as generic feet. Show joints and supporting structure where they affect the silhouette.

The Crescent sofa now uses four dark rectangular blocks, each 0.48 × 0.72 × 0.56 studs, recessed under the walnut frame. Their bottoms are at zero; vertical rays at all four mounting points intersect the base below the 0.72-stud support top. See `support-verification.json` and the updated Blender render.

Before catalog release, inspect seat contact, foot contact, room clearance, collision proxies and material scale in Play. A successful mesh upload is only the import check. Rugs need believable thickness and contact; tabletop decor needs a supported base; patterns need consistent physical scale across differently sized pieces.
