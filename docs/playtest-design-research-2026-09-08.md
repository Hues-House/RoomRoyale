# Hillside Market playtest design research

Researched September 8, 2026 UTC, September 7 in Los Angeles. This is a source-based design brief for the next playtest. I read official Roblox guidance and first-party game descriptions. I did not play the reference games or inspect their current layouts, so the recommendations below are design judgments, not observed gameplay results.

The immediate direction is an open, easy shopping circuit with an unmistakable checkout, recognizable furniture piled high on the cart, and optional routes that let confident riders earn special pieces. The September 7 feedback supersedes the earlier modest-jump proposal. The [current refinement plan](refinement-plan-2026-09-08.md) records the accepted next steps. Preserve the agreed shared Jump/Drift gesture, automatic stacking, cargo ownership through crashes, repeat checkout trips, and `Shop -> Style -> Judge` round.

The reference set has documented reach. Roblox's December 2025 review lists Grow a Garden among its three most-searched games and reports a 21.6 million concurrent-player record in 2025. It also identifies Dress to Impress as a creative experience sought by tablet users. Those are dated popularity signals, not September 2026 active-player counts or proof that any particular design caused success. [Roblox's 2025 review](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/12/roblox-replay-decoded-search-style)

| Reference | What the source documents | What Room Royale should borrow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grow a Garden | Its creator describes buying seeds, planting, watching growth, harvesting, and showing finds to friends. [Creator's game description](https://www.roblox.com/games/126884695634066/Grow-a-Garden) | Make progress physical and visible. A shopper should recognize the sofa moving off its display and into a growing pile. The cart is the shopping phase's shareable result. |
| Dress to Impress | Its creator emphasizes making outfits, posing on a runway, competing with friends, and voting on favorite outfits. [Creator's game description](https://www.roblox.com/games/15101393044/Dress-To-Impress) | Keep the theme and destination clear while selection and self-expression provide variety. Treat checkout and the later room reveal as performances worth watching. |
| Hello Kitty Cafe and Color or Die | Roblox documents a directional trail and highlighted controls in Hello Kitty Cafe, and a core-mechanic sign directly in the starting sightline in Color or Die. These are documented teaching examples; no current popularity claim is made. [Onboarding techniques](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques) | Put useful instructions where the player is already looking. Start with a visible tube and one highlighted nearby item; show Jump/Drift instruction when approaching a practice ramp. |
| Mario Kart World | Nintendo documents drift charge stages, release boosts, ramp tricks with landing rewards, and charge jumps that connect into other traversal moves. This is the already-selected handling reference, outside Roblox. [Nintendo's driving tips](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/get-a-head-start-on-mario-kart-world-with-a-few-tips/) | Let a rider read charge, takeoff, airtime, and landing as distinct moments. Preserve momentum through a successful sequence. Optional routes should reward a move the player has already had room to practice. |

Roblox recommends prioritizing information by the player's current action, using consistent visual language, and limiting competing bright or moving elements. It explicitly treats sound, animation, and changing controls as feedback that tells players their action worked. This supports more useful floating signs with a clear hierarchy, rather than making every object equally prominent. [UI and UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)

| Playtest issue | Recommended change | Concrete review in the next session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confusing, segmented market | Use one broad continuous main circuit. Place checkout on an open plaza visible from spawn and reconnect every outside branch to the circuit. Keep big furniture early along the circuit and small finishing pieces near checkout. Use low displays and gentle changes in elevation. Build two optional courses with visible reward pedestals, generous landings, and a quick return after a miss. Required shopping must work without jumping. | A new player points out checkout before moving, collects three pieces, and returns without verbal directions. Two loaded carts can pass on the main route. At each branch, the player can identify the normal route and the optional reward route. |
| 2. Tiny replacement items and no spectacle | Reuse the actual item's appearance, proportions, color, and material. A visual clone is fine if it looks like the same piece. Start with moderate uniform reduction, roughly 75-90% where the silhouette permits, then validate against the cart and camera. Stack broad pieces low, tall pieces visibly upward, and small pieces around or above them. Give additions a brief flight, landing compression, and settling wobble. Keep cargo visual motion separate from collision and collection records. | Pick a sofa, lamp, and plant. Recognize each on the cart from another rider's view, then follow those same silhouettes into the tube. A full cart is visibly fuller than a half-full cart. The pile does not hide the road. |
| 3. Unclear UI and route | Make `CHECKOUT` plus a tube icon the largest floating sign. Repeat directional checkout signs where branches reconnect. Give each department one medium sign with a recognizable item icon and a short label. Keep item prompts nearby and show one next action. Use a quiet top HUD for theme, time, and cart space. Pair color with shape, icon, or words. | With sound muted, identify checkout, the nearest collectible, cart capacity, and the current theme. On a phone-sized viewport, the signs and controls remain readable without filling the driving view. |
| 4. No mouse camera movement or clear touch controls | Restore independent look control. Prefer the standard Roblox orbit behavior where compatible; otherwise implement mouse orbit and free-area touch drag in the chase camera. Keep the horizon steady. Pause automatic recentering after deliberate look input and ease it back while driving. Touch needs left-thumb steering, a reachable Jump/Drift button, and open right-side space for camera drag. A finger pressing an action button must not also turn the camera. | Drive and look sideways at the same time on PC. Repeat steering, camera drag, and charged-jump release on touch. Test releasing a finger off the button, opening the menu, and losing window focus. Nothing stays held. |
| 5. Delayed ejection and cart falling through the floor | Treat first qualified structural impact, ejection, and recovery as one event. Keep floor support and collision active for the cart throughout the rider's flight. Use the last validated supported position when the cart needs recovery. Preserve the load and prevent another ejection during recovery protection. Give immediate impact feedback while authority resolves the event. | Film a full-speed wall impact and a broadside drift impact. The visible hit and ejection belong to the same moment. A glancing scrape stays a scrape. Repeat at a ramp edge and under shelving; neither rider nor cart finishes below the floor, and cargo count remains unchanged. |
| 6. Rolly handling and weak jumps | Increase ordinary grip and settle unwanted yaw quickly. Reserve sustained sideways travel for intentional drift. Limit body roll and cargo lean independently of the actual steering response. Target the user's requested 3-4 times current charged-jump apex, with reduced gravity during cart airtime, modest aim correction, a readable Superman-like rider pose, and a firm landing that retains forward motion. | Compare the same measured flat-ground jump before and after, empty and fully loaded. Record apex, airtime, landing distance, and ability to steer after touchdown. A novice completes an ordinary shopping lap without repeated left-right correction. |

These layout choices adapt Roblox's greyboxing advice about orientation, limiting choices at intersections, traversable scale, and repeated playtesting. Its example is a laser-tag map, so its three-lane combat layout and exact corridor dimensions should not be copied into a shopping game. Measure clearance using this cart, its maximum load, its turning radius, and the chase camera. [Greybox your environment](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/greybox-your-environment)

Roblox's Classic camera supports orbit and pitch. `CameraType.Scriptable` overrides the default camera scripts, so a custom chase camera must provide the missing behavior. Roblox also recommends input-specific prompts, unobscured controls, suitable contrast, and testing through device emulation. The exact control layout above is a proposed Room Royale design, not a platform requirement. [Camera behavior](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/camera), [cross-platform development](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/cross-platform)

Jump height must be tuned as height. For a ballistic jump, `height = launchSpeed^2 / (2 * gravity)`. At unchanged gravity, a 3-4 times higher apex requires about 1.73-2 times the launch speed. Reduced gravity also raises the apex, so increasing launch speed and reducing gravity independently can overshoot the target. Measure the real cart arc, including support release and landing, instead of judging the impulse number alone. Apply the float to cart airtime rather than changing gravity for the entire game.

The sound pass should give shopping a physical character and make success recognizable. Roblox supports positional audio for world events, non-positional feedback for the local player, and free-to-use professional audio through the Creator Store. The existing `Sound` API supports distance falloff and grouped volume control; its documentation now labels it legacy, with newer audio objects available. Replacing sample quality does not require migrating the entire audio system. [Audio guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/audio), [sound objects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/sound/objects)

| Event | Sound direction to audition | Timing and mix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driving | Soft wheel rumble and a restrained shopping-cart rattle | Track travel speed; fade at rest. Keep this below action feedback and reduce it in the air. |
| Item collected | Brief lift whoosh, then wood knock, soft cushion thump, or light ceramic clink matched to the piece | Sync the landing sound to the item meeting the stack. Avoid glass-breaking cues for successful collection. |
| Jump and drift charge | A short spring compression with a rising tone; distinct ready accents for earned stages | Stop on cancellation. Full charge should sound ready without becoming a continuous alarm. |
| Takeoff and airtime | A clean launch pop and light rushing air | Let the world breathe during flight. Avoid loud repeating jump sounds. |
| Landing and crash | A rounded suspension thump for landing; a separate cart impact and comic rider-flight cue for a crash | Scale landing weight gently. Trigger once per event and keep normal landings distinct from ejections. |
| Checkout | Spatial suction at the tube, individual item swishes, then a short register/chime resolution | Build the sequence with the visible load. Play the success resolution only when the checkout is accepted. Lower other sounds briefly so the haul is the focus. |

Audition actual clips on laptop speakers, phone speakers, and headphones before selecting asset IDs. Use a few restrained variations per repeated action and cap overlapping copies. A larger item should feel heavier through attack and body, not simply through louder volume. These are sound-design recommendations; no replacement clips were selected or listened to in this research pass.

For tonight's iteration, first run one silent first-time shopping lap, then one loaded lap with audio, one outside course, and repeated wall-impact recoveries. Capture a short clip and the player's immediate comment after each. Change one handling variable at a time after the functional fixes. The acceptance targets in this document are proposed checks, not completed results.

The follow-up pump park request fits the same principle. Velosolutions describes a shared rolling line that beginners can ride through and skilled riders can jump, with speed and momentum creating further challenges. Its design guidance also relates turn radius to expected approach speed and gives beginners room in the bottom of a bank. [Velosolutions pump tracks](https://velosolutions.com/asphalt-pump-tracks/), [Velosolutions design guidance](https://velosolutions.com/velosolutions-pump-track-standards/)

The prototype practice park therefore has a continuous 24-stud-wide loop, 42-stud inside bend radius, low rollers, and a separate jump line with a broad landing. A solid floor beneath both routes makes missed landings quick to recover. Its entry connects directly to Hillside Market, while the builder accepts an origin transform for later neighborhood placement. These dimensions and gentle bank angles are cart-playtest values, not real-world track specifications. Test one ordinary rolling lap, one lap using jumps and airborne dives, and a trick-line landing with the camera moving. Random tricks should animate the rider while keeping steering and camera direction predictable.
